# Project Brief — Teedee Corporate Website and Website CMS

**Version:** 0.1 | **Date:** 24 November 2026 | **Status:** Draft

## 1. Executive summary

Teedee needs a stronger, company-owned digital presence that explains what the company does, communicates its capabilities accurately, establishes confidence and creates a straightforward path for enquiries.

The proposed solution is a distinctive, modern corporate website supported by a custom CMS. The public website is the visitor experience; the CMS is the internal publishing product that allows authorised company personnel to keep the website accurate and current.

The project builds on an existing Next.js repository and an existing Netlify deployment configuration. It is therefore an upgrade and extension of an existing software product, not a greenfield website.

## 2. Strategic positioning

The existing archived planning pack uses the proposed brand line **“Secure. Power. Automate.”** and describes Teedee as a practical systems partner for safer, powered and better-connected spaces. These are useful starting points, not automatically approved marketing claims. The company should confirm the brand line, positioning and service terminology before publication.

The site should translate technical capability into customer understanding:

- What problem can Teedee help address?
- What services or systems may be relevant?
- What information should a customer prepare?
- What does engagement with the company look like?
- How can a visitor make an enquiry?

## 3. Business context and opportunity

A well-engineered website can serve as a durable corporate asset: a consistent company profile, a discoverable source of verified information, an enquiry channel and a place to publish approved evidence of work.

A CMS adds operational value by allowing designated company users to maintain routine content. The benefit depends on an appropriate publishing workflow, clear ownership and practical training—not simply the presence of an admin dashboard.

## 4. Primary audiences — hypotheses to validate

| Audience | Likely need | Website job |
|---|---|---|
| Homeowners / property owners | Understand relevant security, power or electrical options | Explain services in plain language and make contact easy |
| Business owners / operators | Assess technical fit and initiate a project discussion | Present credible capabilities, process and enquiry route |
| Property / facility managers | Understand possible system upgrades or integrated solutions | Explain scope, planning considerations and how to request an assessment |
| Institutions / contractors / project partners | Review capability and contact a potential delivery partner | Present accurate service information and a clear business contact path |

These are planning hypotheses drawn from the existing project materials. They must be checked against Teedee's actual customers, markets and service strategy.

## 5. User problems to solve

1. A visitor may not know which service is relevant to their need.
2. Technical descriptions may list equipment without explaining customer outcomes.
3. Visitors need evidence and factual information before trusting a technical service provider.
4. Contact paths can be difficult to find or may not clearly explain what information is useful.
5. A visually rich site can still fail if mobile usability, accessibility, performance or content clarity are weak.
6. Company staff may need routine website changes but lack a governed publishing tool.

## 6. Product vision

Deliver a high-quality corporate website and a purpose-built CMS that work as one coherent product. The public experience should be distinctive and immersive where appropriate; the CMS should be equally considered as a professional software interface, prioritising clarity, safe publishing and ease of use.

Quality means more than visual polish. The system must also be accurate, accessible, secure, responsive, performant, testable, maintainable and easy to hand over.

## 7. Guiding principles

- Accuracy before persuasion: do not invent company facts, credentials, project history, client names, warranties or statistics.
- Distinctiveness without spectacle: motion, 3D and video must serve the message and remain optional where accessibility or performance requires it.
- Content is a product asset: structure it for reuse, maintenance and search visibility.
- CMS permissions matter: content editing must not equal infrastructure administration.
- Company ownership from the beginning: source code and production services should be owned by Teedee.
- Build on the existing repository: inspect before replacing or restructuring.
- Modular engineering: clear boundaries and reusable components without premature microservices.
- Evidence-led delivery: decisions, tests, approvals and changes must be recorded.
- Future-aware, not future-bloated: consider future Teedee products without implementing or prematurely coupling them to this project.

## 8. Wider digital roadmap context

The larger vision may later include:

- **Teedee Ops:** project delivery, field reporting, surveys, inventory, tools, QA and internal operational workflows.
- **Teedee Connect:** customer-facing project visibility, released documents, approvals and service requests.

For this engagement, these are future discovery tracks only. No Ops or Connect features, apps, databases, interfaces or production workflows are included in the current website/CMS fee.

## 9. Desired outcomes

- A coherent, approved corporate profile.
- Clear service and audience information architecture.
- A visually distinctive and responsive public site.
- Useful, approved imagery and video.
- A working enquiry path.
- A secure, usable CMS with agreed publishing capabilities.
- Technical and content discoverability foundations.
- Company-owned infrastructure and a documented handover path.

## 10. What this brief does not decide

This brief does not yet lock the final visual design, domain, CMS roles, exact CMS feature depth, data model, database provider, dynamic rendering strategy, media-production supplier, privacy text or recurring infrastructure budget. Those are discovery and approval items tracked in the related documents.
