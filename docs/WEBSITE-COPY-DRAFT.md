# 1. Copy strategy

## Working positioning

TeeDee Edge Automations helps homes, workplaces and organisations plan, install and maintain the technology systems their spaces depend on.

The current website describes four areas of work: security and surveillance, network infrastructure, power and energy systems, and integrated technology. The message should make the practical work clear before introducing broader language about automation or intelligent infrastructure.

## Core message

**Technology should work together, where it matters.**

TeeDee works across the systems that help a space stay secure, connected and powered. The job begins with understanding the site and its requirements, then planning and delivering a solution that people can use and maintain.

## Proposed brand line

**Secure. Power. Automate.**

Use as a short brand signature only after the company approves it. It should not replace a clear explanation of the services.

## Supporting messages

1. **Start with the site.** The environment, people, equipment and intended use shape the right solution.
2. **Think beyond individual devices.** Security, connectivity and power need to work reliably as part of the wider environment.
3. **Make the work understandable.** Clear scope, considered installation and useful handover matter as much as switching equipment on.

## Voice

- **Capable, not boastful.** State what the company does. Avoid claims such as leading, best, world-class or industry-leading without evidence.
- **Technical, not difficult.** Use correct technical language, then explain what it means for the customer.
- **Practical, not generic.** Talk about sites, coverage, cabling, power requirements, configuration, testing and handover where relevant.
- **Human, not overfamiliar.** Sound like a thoughtful technical team. No forced slang or exaggerated friendliness.

## Audience hypothesis

The website should speak first to decision-makers responsible for a property, workplace, facility or project. Potential readers include property owners, business owners, facility and operations managers, project leads and procurement teams. The final priority audience needs owner confirmation.

## Why the work starts with understanding

**Eyebrow:** BEFORE WE RECOMMEND EQUIPMENT

# The site comes first.

A good recommendation depends on more than a product list. We need to understand the layout, the people who will use the system, existing infrastructure, operating conditions and what the installation is expected to do.

That gives the project a clearer starting point and helps avoid decisions based on assumptions.

**Three principles**

- **Understand the environment.** The site and its requirements guide the solution.
- **Plan the installation.** Equipment, placement, connections and practical constraints need to be considered together.
- **Make handover useful.** The people responsible for the system should understand what has been installed and how to use it.

## FAQ

### What services does TeeDee provide?

The current service areas presented on this website are security and surveillance, network infrastructure, power and energy systems, and integrated technology. The exact services available for a project depend on its requirements and the company's confirmed delivery scope.

### Can you assess an existing installation?

Yes, subject to service availability and the nature of the issue. Tell us what system is installed, where it is located and what is not working as expected. The team can review the request and advise on a suitable next step.

### Do you work with homes and businesses?

TeeDee can review enquiries for residential and business environments. The right service and project scope depend on the site, requirements and location. Confirm the company's preferred customer segments before publishing this answer.

### Can you handle more than one system at a site?

Where the project requires it and the relevant services are within TeeDee's delivery scope, different systems can be considered together. Share the site requirements so the team can assess the work.

### What information should I include in an enquiry?

A project location, the type of property or site, the system you need, a short description of the requirement and your preferred timeline are useful starting points. You can include photographs or plans if they help explain the site.

### How do I get a quotation?

Start by sending the project details. The team will review the information and advise whether a site assessment or further discussion is needed before a scope and quotation can be prepared. Do not promise a free assessment or a fixed quotation turnaround unless confirmed.

### Do you provide maintenance and support?

Please contact the team with the system details and the support required. Available maintenance and support arrangements should be confirmed for each service and location.

# 6. Editorial and implementation notes

1. The website should lead with the work TeeDee actually performs, not abstract claims about innovation.
2. Use "TeeDee" after the first full company-name reference. Keep "TeeDee Edge Automations LTD" for formal contexts.
3. Use "site" when referring to the physical environment. Use "project" for the agreed body of work. Use "system" for the installed combination of equipment and configuration.
4. Explain acronyms on first use. Do not overload the public site with model numbers or engineering jargon.
5. Use sentence case for headings and buttons. Avoid all-caps paragraphs.
6. Buttons should describe the next action. Do not use "Learn more" where a more specific label is possible.
7. Keep form labels visible. Do not rely on placeholders as labels.
8. Give every field a clear purpose. Do not request sensitive technical drawings or personal information unless the company has approved the handling process.
9. Keep the form confirmation specific: "Your enquiry has been received. The TeeDee team will review the details and contact you using the information provided." Publish a response-time promise only when one is agreed.
10. No invented metrics, testimonials, project examples, certifications, guarantees or company history.
11. Use the approved TeeDee brand book for visual implementation. Current proposed palette: Signal Red #B32632, Signal Orange #E57A2F, Graphite #17191D and Warm Paper #F4F2ED. Proposed typography: Manrope for display, Inter for body and IBM Plex Mono for technical labels. Confirm final approval before treating these as locked brand standards.
