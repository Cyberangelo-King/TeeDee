# Project Roadmap and Delivery Milestones

**Version:** 0.1 | **Prepared:** 24 November 2026 | **Status:** Provisional target plan

## 1. Planning horizon

The working target is to complete the agreed corporate website and custom Website CMS by the end of December 2026, with the company beginning public rollout and sharing in January 2027.

This is a provisional delivery plan, not a guarantee. The schedule depends on prompt company decisions, verified content, approved images/video, domain access, account provisioning, the CMS deployment proof of concept and controlled scope.

## 2. Delivery strategy

Deliver in gated phases. Do not start building a CMS on an untested hosting assumption or fill a website with unapproved content simply to meet a date.

| Phase | Target window | Focus | Exit gate |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 — Foundation and agreement | 24–27 Nov | Charter, scope, stakeholder roles, current-state audit, client-owned access plan | CEO confirms scope, approvers and account owner |
| 1 — Discovery and content | 27 Nov–3 Dec | Company facts, service catalogue, page requirements, CMS workflow, asset and domain requirements | Content pack and CMS MVP agreed |
| 2 — UX, brand and architecture | 30 Nov–7 Dec | Visual direction, website/CMS flows, architecture decision, rendering/hosting proof of concept | Design direction and technical approach approved |
| 3 — Website and CMS engineering | 8–18 Dec | Refine website, implement CMS foundation, auth, content models, media and publishing | Core public and CMS journeys demonstrable |
| 4 — Content, SEO and integration | 19–23 Dec | Approved copy/media, content migration, SEO/AEO/GEO implementation, enquiry delivery | Content and integration review passed |
| 5 — Quality and hardening | 24–28 Dec | Responsive, accessibility, performance, security, form, publishing and recovery checks | Release candidate accepted for final review |
| 6 — Launch, training and handover | 29–31 Dec | Domain, HTTPS, production release, CMS orientation, final handover documentation | Teedee signs off launch and receives operating materials |

Some phases overlap deliberately. The developer must keep dependencies visible and avoid hiding schedule risk.

## 3. Milestones and deliverables

### M0 — Project baseline
- Initial project documentation prepared.
- Scope and payment terms reconciled with written agreement.
- CEO and company account owner identified.
- Existing website/repository findings documented.

### M1 — Approved content and product baseline
- Legal/public company name, service list and contact facts approved.
- Website page list confirmed.
- CMS MVP content types, roles and workflow confirmed.
- Media availability and permissions documented.
- Domain and account decisions approved.

### M2 — Design and technical proof
- Website and CMS visual direction reviewed.
- Next.js rendering/deployment path tested.
- Database/Auth/Storage provider and project ownership confirmed if required.
- Data model and access policy approach reviewed.

### M3 — Working product
- Public website routes implemented/refined.
- CMS sign-in, permissions and agreed content operations work.
- Published content reaches the website correctly.
- Media and enquiry paths work.

### M4 — Release candidate
- Approved content integrated.
- Discoverability foundations applied.
- Accessibility, performance, security and functional tests completed.
- Known issues and deferred work documented.

### M5 — Production and knowledge transfer
- Primary domain resolves correctly with HTTPS.
- Production build and smoke tests pass.
- Teedee can access and operate company accounts.
- CMS training/owner orientation completed.
- Final handover documents reflect the actual system.

## 4. Critical-path dependencies

1. CEO confirms scope, approver and CMS owner.
2. Company creates/controls administrative email and recovery.
3. Company confirms repository transfer/visibility and hosting ownership.
4. Company selects domain and approves purchase.
5. Company supplies verified copy, contact information and media.
6. Architecture proof confirms the CMS can be hosted and public content can be rendered correctly.
7. CMS scope remains within the agreed MVP.
8. Company reviews builds and approves production launch without avoidable delay.

Any delay in a critical dependency may move the target completion date. The roadmap should be re-baselined rather than compressing security, accuracy or QA.

## 5. Future roadmap — separate engagements

The following sequence is strategic only:

| Future stage | Product | Discovery objective | Delivery status |
|---|---|---|---|
| Future 1 | Teedee Ops | Observe workflows, map project/site delivery, survey, reporting, inventory, tools, QA and permissions | Not in current scope |
| Future 2 | Teedee Connect | Define customer access, released project updates, documents, approvals and service requests | Not in current scope |
| Future 3 | Shared platform capabilities | Validate shared identity, APIs, notifications, audit, files, reporting and integrations | Not in current scope |
| Future 4 | Automation / intelligence | Identify repeatable processes and measurable ROI before automation or AI | Not in current scope |

These products require their own discovery, PRDs, TRDs, risk reviews, budget and approval. They must not delay or silently enlarge the current website/CMS engagement.

## 6. Status reporting

Use the following statuses:
- Not started
- In progress
- Blocked
- In review
- Done (with evidence)
- Deferred
- Cancelled

At each milestone, record completed work, outstanding items, risks, decisions required and schedule impact.
