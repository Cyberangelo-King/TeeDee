# Market, User and Competitor Research Brief

## Purpose
Validate the website's commercial message, user needs, trust signals, information architecture and interaction priorities before finalising design and copy. Research must inform decisions rather than become a decorative report.

## Research questions
### Market and category
- How do credible Nigerian and international integrated-systems providers present CCTV/security, solar/inverter, electrical, access control, networking and automation services?
- Which customer segments do they explicitly target?
- How do they explain assessment, installation, commissioning, maintenance and aftercare?
- What proof do they present: project photography, case studies, certifications, partners, reviews, process documentation?
- What conversion routes are used: WhatsApp, calls, forms, site assessment requests, quotation requests?

### User needs
- What information do homeowners need before contacting an installer?
- What do commercial buyers and facility managers need to assess provider credibility?
- What makes a technical services website confusing or untrustworthy?
- What are the common objections: cost uncertainty, equipment choice, coverage, power reliability, installation disruption, maintenance, support?
- Which information should be shown before a visitor submits an enquiry?

### Competitor experience
Benchmark a representative mix of:
- Nigerian security/CCTV integrators;
- solar/inverter providers;
- electrical and building-services companies;
- access-control/networking providers;
- international integrated building technology companies.

Do not assume every competitor is direct. Classify direct, adjacent and aspirational references.

## Benchmark dimensions
Score each site from 1–5 and attach screenshot/evidence:
1. Brand clarity;
2. First-screen message;
3. Navigation and findability;
4. Service taxonomy;
5. Technical explanation quality;
6. Photography and video quality;
7. Proof and trust;
8. Project/case-study presentation;
9. Contact/enquiry friction;
10. Mobile experience;
11. Accessibility;
12. Performance;
13. Motion quality;
14. Content freshness and SEO.

## Output format
Create `docs/RESEARCH_REPORT.md` with:
- research scope and date;
- methods and sources;
- audience hypotheses;
- top user questions;
- competitor matrix with URLs;
- screenshots or screenshot references;
- patterns worth adopting;
- weaknesses TeeDee can avoid;
- differentiators that are actually supportable;
- implications for page structure;
- implications for copy;
- implications for visual direction;
- confidence level and limitations.

## Research integrity
- Cite source URLs and dates.
- Distinguish observed facts from interpretation.
- Do not claim user interviews occurred unless they did.
- If direct interviews are not possible, label findings as desk-research hypotheses.
- Do not invent competitor features, pricing or customer feedback.
- Do not copy competitor copy or assets.
- Do not recommend claims TeeDee cannot substantiate.

## Practical user validation
If possible, conduct 5–8 short interviews with people representing:
- homeowners/landlords;
- SME/business operators;
- property/facility managers;
- institutional/contractor buyers.

Ask:
1. What problem would make you look for this service?
2. What would you need to know before contacting a provider?
3. What would make you trust or distrust a provider's website?
4. What would you expect to happen after submitting an enquiry?
5. Which words or service descriptions are unclear?

Record anonymised themes, not sensitive personal details.
